# Top-layer classification (Prop. 6.1, m = 7, chart form `ChartClassification`): proved in Lean

## 1. Result (v17)

**`ChartClassification` is proved in Lean. It is kernel-checked and uses the standard axioms only**
(`Jacobian/ChartProof/`, generated by `certgen/chartproof/`). `ChartClassification` is the m = 7 case
of Proposition 6.1 in the normalized chart (`a₀ = b₁₀ = 1`, `a₇³b₀² = 1`), which is the form the main
theorem uses. The eliminant 𝒲 of degree 35 and the cases m = 3, 5 are not formalized.

```lean
theorem chartClassification_holds (L : Type*) [Field L] [CharZero L] : ChartClassification L

/-- Main Theorem 1.1 (branch (a,b), degree pair (72,108)), unconditional. -/
theorem main_theorem {L : Type*} [Field L] [CharZero L] :
    ¬ ∃ (P Q : MvPolynomial (Fin 2) L) (lam : L), lam ≠ 0 ∧ NewtonNF2 P Q ∧
      jac P Q = MvPolynomial.C lam * MvPolynomial.X 0 ^ 2 :=
  main_theorem_of_chart (chartClassification_holds L)
```

```
'BranchAb.chartClassification_holds' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]
'BranchAb.main_theorem' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]
```

These are in `logs/chartproof_axioms.log`. The NewtonNF2 form of Theorem 1.1 is therefore a single
unconditional kernel-checked theorem. GGHV Proposition 4.3, which connects it to the Jacobian conjecture,
remains outside the formal statement. So does the `a₈,₁₆ = 0` refinement, which is computer algebra.

The statement `ChartClassification` is the one shipped in `Jacobian/BranchAbChart.lean`, unchanged. The
new modules only prove it.

## 2. The proof

The key idea is a change of coordinates that turns the chart system into a truncated binomial series.

1. **Reversed coordinates.** Let `y_i = a_{7−i}/a₇` for `1 ≤ i ≤ 6` and `y₇ = 1/a₇`, and write
   `(1 + y₁v + … + y₇v⁷)^{3/2} = Σ S_k(y) v^k`. Then `2k S_k = Σ_{i=1..7} (5i − 2k) y_i S_{k−i}`.
   Divided by `a₇`, the equation `E_{17−k}` is exactly the k-th step of this recursion with
   `b_{10−k}` in place of `S_k`. This gives the following (`Stage1.lean`, 17 steps):
   * `b_{10−k} = S_k(y)` for `k ≤ 10`;
   * `S₁₁ = … = S₁₆ = 0`;
   * `a₇³b₀² = 1` becomes `S₁₀² = y₇³`.
2. **Orbit relations.** Six relations of weights 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7 (with `y_i` of weight `i`) satisfy
   `y₇³·g ∈ (S₁₁, …, S₁₆)` over ℚ, with explicit certificates (`Stage2_*.lean`). They are
   `y₄ = H₄`, `y₅ = H₅`, `y₆ = H₆` and `y₇ = H₇`, each `H_i ∈ ℚ[y₁, y₂, y₃]` of weight `i`, together
   with one relation `y₂y₃ = …` of weight 5 and one relation `y₃² = …` of weight 6. So the five torus
   orbits are cut out in the weighted plane `P(1,2,3)` by a curve of weight 5 and a curve of weight 6,
   and `5·6/(1·2·3) = 5`.
3. **Chart `t = y₂/y₁²`, `s = y₃/y₁³`** (`Stage3.lean`). `y₁ ≠ 0` because `g₇ ≡ c·y₇ mod y₁`. The
   Sylvester resultant in `s` of the two remaining relations is `κ·m(t)` with `m` irreducible of degree
   5, and `s = q₃(t)`. The normalization `S₁₀² = y₇³` then fixes `y₁ = σ(t)²/q₇(t)³ mod m`, so the torus
   is fixed with no 7th root. With `w = ω(t)` this gives `R(w) = 0` and `y_i = Y_i(w)`.
4. **Back to `(a, b)`** (`Stage4.lean`). `a_j = P_j(w)` and `b_k = Q_k(w)`, exactly the coordinates in
   the statement of `ChartClassification`.

## 3. How the kernel checks it

* **Certificate steps.** There are 111 steps. 105 of them are
  `lc_zero ctx g [(c₁,e₁),…] (by decide +kernel) ⟨…⟩` (`Reflect.lean`). The other six, one per orbit
  relation, check `y₇³·g = e` with `eq_of_toPolyK` and cancel `y₇³` using `y₇ ≠ 0`. In both cases the
  kernel evaluates the verified normalizer of `Lean.Grind.CommRing` (`combine_k`, `mulMon_k`,
  `mulConst_k`, `Poly.beq'`), plus recursor-defined `mulK`, `powK` and `toPolyK`, whose soundness
  (`denote_toPolyK`) is proved from core's lemmas.
* **No compiled evaluation.** There is no `native_decide` and no `Lean.ofReduceBool`.
* **Stage-2 batching.** The Stage-2 certificates are split into batches of at most 9,000 monomial
  products, one declaration each. Section 5 shows that core `Expr.toPoly` on a single large identity
  runs out of memory; batching and recursor-defined multiplication avoid that (measured on the whole
  `g₄` certificate, 27,747 products, as a single check: with core `Expr.toPoly` the process is killed out
  of memory after 44 s; `toPolyK` without batching succeeds in 43 s but peaks at 5.6 GB; with batching the
  whole `Stage2_g4` module peaks at 3.3 GB).
* **SymPy.** Every certificate was also verified by exact SymPy expansion before it was emitted.

## 5. History: why the original formulations resisted certificates

The measurements below (mod 32003, Singular) are from the v8–v13 packaging. They explain why
`ChartClassification` was a hypothesis until v17. The difficulty was the choice of coordinates. In the
`(a, b)` coordinates, and in the `a₀ = b₀ = 1` normalization, the degenerate Davenport–Stothers
components sit where the saturation needs high powers: `a₇^7`–`a₇^8`, at weight 61. In the reversed
coordinates `y`, the same components sit at `y₇ = 0` and need only `y₇³`, at weight ≤ 28. Each of
the six v17 certificates has 341–694 cofactor terms over ℚ, against 503,254 cofactor terms (mod p)
for the sub-case `a₁ = 0` alone in the chart `a₀ = b₁₀ = 1`: roughly a thousand times fewer.

### Formulation `a₀ = b₀ = 1` (a-variables only, weights 1..7)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Weighted Bézout number of `g₁₁..g₁₆` | 1144 (true solutions: 5 weighted-projective points) |
| Minimal primes of `I = (g₁₁..g₁₆)` | 6: three degenerate (a₇ = 0; the m = 1, 3, 5 solutions), three for the true orbits (the K₅ orbit splits mod p) |
| Homogeneous GB of `I` | 1906 elements, 417,177 terms, max weight 65 |
| `J = I : a₇^∞` | 36 elements, each with ≤ 6 terms, weights 5–13 |
| Power of a₇ needed per element of J (`a₇^k s ∈ I`) | k = 7 or 8, i.e. certificates at weight 5 + 56 = 61 |
| Chart `a₇ = 1` (needs 7th roots in L) | 35 points; `lift` cofactors 3,060,689 terms, degree 21 |

### Formulation `a₀ = b₁₀ = 1` (bilinear, 16–17 variables)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| `a₁ = 0` | unit ideal in 4 s; `lift` cofactors 503,254 terms, degree 15 |
| Buchberger trace for `a₁ = 0` (sugar strategy, mod p) | 2,065 basis elements, 223,351 reduction steps, 3.08 M monomial products; pruned to the elements needed for `1`: 1,771 elements, 2.12 M products |
| The same trace over ℚ | coefficients grow to 2,722 digits after 929 of 2,065 elements |
| Chart `a₇³ b₀² = 1` (the one in Lean) | the mod-p std had not finished after 30 min in the `(a,b)` coordinates; in the `y` coordinates (affine chart `y₇ = 1`) the GB takes 1–3 s |

### Lean throughput (single-identity checks)

| Check | Size | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `ring` on one product identity | 400 × 71 terms, 14,068 output terms | timeout at 1200 s |
| kernel reflection on core `Expr.toPoly` | same identity | out of memory at 699 s |
| v17: recursor-defined `toPolyK`, one Stage-2 module (5–10 batches of ≤ 9,000 monomial products each) | 27,747–56,999 products per orbit relation | 105–335 s per module, peak RSS 3.3–5.4 GB |

## 6. External evidence (kept; now superseded by the Lean proof)

`certgen/chart_cas.py` and `check_chart_cas.sh` still encode the same system for Singular. Their result
(five points, the K₅ conjugates) agrees with the Lean theorem.

The prototype `experimental/CertCheck.lean` used `native_decide`. It is not used by the v17 proof.
