# Branch (c): the chart T₂ ≠ 0 is empty in characteristic 0 (one-prime argument)

Status: proof on paper plus machine-checked finite inputs (exact over K₅ and over 𝔽_p). This is not Lean.
The step from `DescentClaimC`'s hypotheses to the conditions used here is `lower_c`'s elimination
(exact K₅ linear algebra in Python). For E₀, E₋₁ and E₋₂ it is not yet in Lean (see §6).

## 1. Setting

- K₅ = ℚ[w]/(R) with R = w⁵ − w⁴ + 3w³ + 3w² + 26. R is irreducible over ℚ (FLINT factorisation over ℤ).
- The parameters and their weights:

  | x | T₁ | T₂ | S₁ | S₂ | R₁ | R₂ | Q |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | coefficient | b₁₁,₂₀ | b₁₂,₂₂ | b₁₁,₂₁ | b₁₂,₂₃ | a₆,₁₃ | a₇,₁₅ | b₁₀,₂₁ |
  | weight | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 |

  Weighted scaling is λ ⋆ x := (λ^{wt(j)} x_j)_j.
- `lower_c`'s conditions are Ω, Ψ, Φ₁, Φ₂, Θ₁, Θ₂, Θ₃ in K₅[x] (`certgen_c/conds_c.json`, md5 `168299d2…`, produced by
  `certgen_c/lower_c.py`, md5 `dcb7894b…`). The five pure rows are not used.
- κ := −o₂/(2o₁), where Ω = o₁S₂² + o₂T₂²S₂ + o₃T₂⁴. Sq := S₂ − κT₂².
- J₆ := (Sq, Ψ, Φ₁, Φ₂, Θ₁, Θ₂, Θ₃).
- Slice at Q = 1, with y := (T₁, T₂, S₁, S₂, R₁, R₂): f⁽¹⁾(y) := f(y, 1) for f ∈ J₆.

**Theorem.** Let L be a field of characteristic 0 and w ∈ L a root of R. There is no x ∈ L⁷ with T₂ ≠ 0 and
Ω = Ψ = Φ₁ = Φ₂ = Θ₁ = Θ₂ = Θ₃ = 0 (coefficients evaluated at w).

In particular, the chart T₂ = 1 of `DescentClaimC` is empty, both for ψ₁ ≠ 0 and ψ₁ = 0.

## 2. Finite inputs, and where each is checked

`padic/check_cert.py` checks all of them without Singular, from `conds_c.json` alone plus the cofactor files.
Singular (`padic/make_cert.py`) only produced the cofactors.

**Exact facts over K₅**

| Input | Statement |
|---|---|
| E1 | o₂² = 4o₁o₃ and o₁ ≠ 0. So Ω = o₁·Sq², and over a field Ω = 0 ⇔ Sq = 0. |
| E2 | Sq, Ψ, Φ₁, Φ₂, Θ₁, Θ₂, Θ₃ are weighted-homogeneous, of weights 2, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7. |
| E3 | None of them contains a monomial Q^k or a constant. So v₀ := (0,0,0,0,0,0,1) is a zero of J₆ over every field, and f⁽¹⁾(0) = 0. |
| E4 | The Jacobian (∂f⁽¹⁾ᵢ/∂y_j)(0) (7 × 6) has rank 5 over K₅. |

Why E4 holds structurally: a monomial of f contributes to the Jacobian at v₀ only if it has the form y_j·Q^k, with
wt(y_j) + 4k = wt(f). Weight 5 then allows only T-entries, weight 6 only S-entries, weight 7 only R-entries. Ψ is the
only weight-5 condition, so the two T-columns have rank ≤ 1. The S and R blocks have rank 2 each.

**Facts at one prime, p = 1000003, r = 806739.** p = 32003, r = 11147 is an independent replicate.

| Input | Statement |
|---|---|
| P0 | R(r) ≡ 0 and R′(r) ≢ 0 mod p. So there is a unique w_p ∈ ℤ_p with R(w_p) = 0 and w_p ≡ r. |
| P1 | Every rational coefficient, in the power basis, of κ and of the coefficients of Ψ, …, Θ₃ has denominator prime to p. Under ι: K₅ → ℚ_p, w ↦ w_p, every f ∈ J₆ therefore lies in ℤ_p[x], and its reduction f̄ ∈ 𝔽_p[x] is obtained by w ↦ r. |
| P2 | For each v ∈ {T₁, T₂, S₁, S₂, R₁, R₂} there is an explicit identity v^{N_v} = Σᵢ c_{v,i}·f̄ᵢ(y, 0) in 𝔽_p[y], with (N_v) = (14, 14, 7, 7, 5, 5). |
| P3 | For each g ∈ {T₁ − c·T₂, S₁, S₂, R₁, R₂, T₂²} there is an explicit identity g = Σᵢ c_{g,i}·f̄⁽¹⁾ᵢ in 𝔽_p[y], with c = 73988 at p = 1000003 and 859 at p = 32003. |

Each identity in P2 and P3 is checked by exact expansion (FLINT `nmod_mpoly`) and by evaluation at 3 random points
with plain Python integers. The negative controls fail: a perturbed P2 cofactor, a perturbed P3 cofactor, and the
32003 certificate checked at the other root 16284.

## 3. Proof

**Step 0: reduction to a p-adic point.**
1. Suppose x ∈ L⁷ is a zero of the conditions with T₂(x) ≠ 0. By E1, x is a zero of J₆.
2. Let k₀ = ℚ(w) ⊂ L. Since R is irreducible, k₀ ≅ K₅.
3. The ideal I ⊂ k₀[x, z] generated by J₆ and zT₂ − 1 has a zero over L, so 1 ∉ I.
4. By the Nullstellensatz, I has a zero over an algebraic closure k̄₀.
5. The map k₀ → ℚ_p, w ↦ w_p, is a field embedding, since R(w_p) = 0 and R is irreducible. It extends to k̄₀ → ℚ̄_p.
6. The image of that zero is a zero X ∈ F⁷ of ι(J₆) with T₂(X) ≠ 0, for some finite extension F/ℚ_p.

**Step 1: primitive weighted rescaling.**
1. Let v be the valuation on ℚ̄_p with v(p) = 1, and m := min { v(X_j)/wt(j) : X_j ≠ 0 }. The set is non-empty because T₂(X) ≠ 0, and m is rational.
2. Take λ ∈ ℚ̄_p with v(λ) = −m, and set X′ := λ ⋆ X.
3. Then v(X′_j) ≥ 0 for all j, with equality for some j.
4. f(X′) = λ^{wt f}·f(X) = 0 for f ∈ J₆ (E2), and T₂(X′) = λ·T₂(X) ≠ 0.
5. X′ lies in O′⁷, where O′ is the valuation ring of a finite extension with residue field k′. Its reduction X̄′ ∈ k′⁷ is non-zero and is a zero of every f̄ (P1).

**Step 2: the reduction lies on the Q-axis, and after rescaling it is the origin of the slice.**
1. If Q̄′ = 0, then f̄ᵢ(ȳ′, 0) = 0 for all i. By P2, ȳ′_v^{N_v} = 0 for every v, so X̄′ = 0, a contradiction. Hence Q′ is a unit.
2. Choose μ with μ⁴ = 1/Q′; μ is a unit. Then X″ := μ ⋆ X′ has Q″ = 1, all coordinates integral, f(X″) = 0 for f ∈ J₆, and T₂(X″) = μλ·T₂(X) ≠ 0.
3. Write y″ for its first six coordinates, so f⁽¹⁾(y″) = 0. Its reduction ȳ″ is a common zero of the f̄⁽¹⁾ᵢ. By P3 it is a zero of T₁ − cT₂, S₁, S₂, R₁, R₂ and T₂², so ȳ″ = 0.
4. So y″ ∈ 𝔪″⁶, where 𝔪″ is the maximal ideal.

**Step 3: the only zero in the residue polydisc of the origin is the origin.**
1. Let B := ℤ_p[[y]]/(f⁽¹⁾ᵢ). The f⁽¹⁾ᵢ are in ℤ_p[y] by P1.
2. **B/pB is small.** B/pB = 𝔽_p[[y]]/(f̄⁽¹⁾ᵢ). By P3 this is a quotient of 𝔽_p[[y]]/(T₁ − cT₂, S₁, S₂, R₁, R₂, T₂²) ≅ 𝔽_p[T₂]/(T₂²), so its dimension is ≤ 2 and it is spanned by the images of 1 and T₂.
3. **B is a finite ℤ_p-module.**
   - B is a Noetherian local ring (a quotient of ℤ_p[[y]]) with maximal ideal 𝔫_B = (p, y).
   - By Krull's intersection theorem, ∩ₙ pⁿB ⊂ ∩ₙ 𝔫_Bⁿ = 0, so B is p-adically separated.
   - ℤ_p is p-adically complete.
   - By the complete Nakayama lemma (Matsumura, *Commutative Ring Theory*, Thm 8.4), 1 and T₂ generate B as a ℤ_p-module.
   - So A := B ⊗_{ℤ_p} ℚ_p satisfies dim_{ℚ_p} A ≤ 2.
4. **The origin already uses up that dimension.**
   - Let 𝔫 := (y)A. Since ℤ_p[[y]]/(y)^N = ℤ_p[y]/(y)^N and localisation is exact, A/𝔫^N ≅ ℚ_p[y]/(f⁽¹⁾, (y)^N) for every N.
   - For N = 2, using f⁽¹⁾(0) = 0 (E3): ℚ_p[y]/(f⁽¹⁾, (y)²) ≅ ℚ_p ⊕ ⟨y⟩/⟨linear parts of f⁽¹⁾ᵢ⟩.
   - Its dimension is 1 + (6 − rank J(0)) = 2. The rank over ℚ_p equals the rank over K₅, which is 5 (E4), because ι is a field embedding.
   - So A → A/𝔫² is a surjection from a space of dimension ≤ 2 onto one of dimension 2. It is an isomorphism, so 𝔫² = 0 in A and every y_j is nilpotent in A.
5. **Hence y″ = 0.**
   - Since y″_j ∈ 𝔪″, evaluation y ↦ y″ defines a continuous ℤ_p-algebra map ℤ_p[[y]] → O″.
   - It kills f⁽¹⁾, so it gives a ℚ_p-algebra map φ: A → F″.
   - Each φ(y_j) = y″_j is nilpotent in the field F″, hence 0.
   - In particular T₂(X″) = 0, which contradicts Step 2. ∎

**Remarks.**
- **Why E4 is needed.** A multiplicity-2 point mod p can be two simple points in characteristic 0. Example: x² − px has the zeros 0 and p, and both reduce to 0. The argument excludes this only because the characteristic-0 Jacobian at v₀ is rank-deficient, which forces the characteristic-0 multiplicity at v₀ to be ≥ 2.
- **Why a single mod-p unit-ideal certificate is not enough.** Such a certificate for the dehomogenised chart only excludes solutions that are integral at p. For example, 1 + p·X is the unit ideal mod p but has the zero X = −1/p. Steps 1–3 are the control at infinity that such a certificate lacks.
- **Existence of a K₅ certificate.** The theorem implies, by the Nullstellensatz, that 1 lies in the chart ideal over K₅. The proof does not produce that certificate.

## 5. What this does not cover

1. **The elimination.** The claim that every solution of `DescentClaimC`'s hypotheses yields a zero of Ω, Ψ, Φ, Θ
   with T₂ = (B 2).coeff 12 = 1 is `lower_c`'s elimination. What `lower_c` asserts:
   - the E₀, E₋₁ and E₋₂ operators are constant over K₅ and injective, so there are no free parameters;
   - the conditions are the left-null combinations plus the pure rows.

   In Lean, only E₄..E₁ are proved (`omega_descent_K5`); E₀, E₋₁ and E₋₂ are pending.
2. **Pen and paper, not Lean.** Step 0 (Nullstellensatz, embeddings into ℚ̄_p) and Step 3 (complete Nakayama, Krull
   intersection) are standard commutative algebra.
3. **The finite inputs** are checked by python-flint (exact) plus random evaluation, not by a proof assistant.

## 6. Status of `DescentClaimC`

`DescentClaimC` is **checked outside Lean**. It holds provided:
- (i) the E₀, E₋₁, E₋₂ elimination of `lower_c` is correct (exact K₅ linear algebra, asserted in code);
- (ii) the argument above is correct (it is under independent review).

It is not a Lean theorem.
